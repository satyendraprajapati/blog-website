Building a data deck and presenting it live are two different skills, and most keyboard-shortcut advice stops at the editing view. Once you're actually in Slide Show mode in front of stakeholders, a different, smaller set of shortcuts matters — because fumbling for the mouse while someone asks "can you go back to the revenue slide" is where a confident readout starts to wobble.

**1. Type a slide number, then press Enter, to jump straight to it.** If someone asks about the churn chart from slide 14, you don't need to click backward through thirteen slides to get there — type `14` and hit `Enter` and you're on it immediately, from anywhere in the deck.

**2. Press `B` or `W` to blank the screen to black or white.** When the conversation wanders off the current slide and you want the room's attention back on you instead of a chart, blanking the screen removes the visual distraction without ending the show. Press the same key again (or click) to bring the slide back exactly where you left it.

**3. Press `Ctrl+H` to hide the mouse pointer immediately.** The on-screen cursor and the small navigation arrows in the corner are useful while you're moving around, but distracting once you've landed on a slide and are talking through it. `Ctrl+H` hides both instantly, and they reappear the moment you move the mouse again.

**4. Press `Ctrl+S` to open the "All Slides" dialog and jump by title.** This is a faster version of the number-then-Enter trick for when you don't remember the slide number but do remember what it's called — it pops up a searchable list of every slide title so you can click straight to it.

**5. Press `Home` or `End` to snap to the first or last slide.** Useful for two common moments: jumping back to your title slide to re-anchor the room after a long tangent, or jumping to a closing summary slide if you're running short on time and need to wrap up without clicking through everything in between.

**6. Press `Esc` to exit the show at any point — it won't walk you through the rest of the deck first.** Clicking past the final slide drops you into a black "end of slide show" screen you then have to click through again. `Esc` exits straight back to the editing view from wherever you are, which is the cleaner way to end early or recover from presenting the wrong file.

None of these replace knowing your material, but they mean the mechanics of moving through the deck never become the thing the room notices.
