excerpt: "Typing in a hex code from memory drifts from the actual brand color over time — the Eyedropper tool lets you sample it directly off a logo or approved swatch instead."
---

A deck built without exact brand hex codes on hand tends to drift: one chart series ends up "close enough" blue, the next report's version is a slightly different close-enough blue, and after a few decks nothing matches. PowerPoint's Eyedropper tool skips the guessing by sampling color directly off a pixel on your screen.

**1. Open it from any color picker.** Select a chart series, shape, or text box, go to its Fill or Font Color menu, and look for the eyedropper icon at the top of the color grid, next to "More Colors." Clicking it turns your cursor into a sampler — click anywhere and that exact color applies.

**2. Sample straight from your brand guide or logo, not from memory.** Paste an image of your company's official color swatches, or the logo itself, onto the slide as a temporary object. Click the Eyedropper, then click a pixel inside the correct swatch — you get the precise brand color without ever looking up or retyping a hex code.

**3. It samples anything on screen, not just the current slide.** The Eyedropper cursor can pick up a color from another window entirely — a PDF brand guidelines document, a webpage, another open PowerPoint file. Arrange the source next to your slide and sample across windows instead of screenshotting a color into the deck first.

**4. Save what you sample to the theme so you only do this once.** After matching a color with the Eyedropper, add it as a custom theme color under Design → Variants → Colors → Customize Colors. Every chart and shape you build afterward can then pick that exact color from the standard palette instead of repeating the sampling step each time.

**5. Remove the temporary swatch object before sharing the deck.** It's easy to forget the pasted logo or color-swatch image once you've finished sampling from it. Delete it — or park it on a hidden slide you never present from — so a stray reference image doesn't end up visible in the file you hand off.

**6. Sample from a vector or source file, not a screenshot of a screenshot.** Re-saving or re-compressing an image can shift its colors slightly through JPEG artifacting. Sample from the original brand guide PDF or logo file when you can, rather than a screenshot someone forwarded three times — the difference is small, but it's exactly the kind of drift this whole approach is meant to eliminate.

The result isn't a visually different chart — it's one that matches the last five decks exactly, instead of approximately, which is the part a stakeholder actually notices when the reports sit side by side.
