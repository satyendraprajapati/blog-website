---

It's easy to open PowerPoint, start building slide one with a chart and a bullet list, and only realize four slides in that the deck doesn't actually flow in the right order. Outline View skips the visual layer entirely and lets you write and rearrange a deck as plain text first — titles and bullet points only, no charts, no fonts, no slide backgrounds — so the argument gets fixed before any formatting work is at risk of being redone.

**1. Turn it on from View > Outline View.** The normal slide thumbnails on the left are replaced with a text outline: each slide's title sits at the top level, and its bullet content is indented underneath. Nothing you type here is styled — it inherits whatever the slide layout already defines, which is exactly the point.

**2. Write every slide title as the finding, not the topic.** Because Outline View shows nothing but titles and text, a weak title — "Q3 Revenue by Region" instead of "West Region Revenue Fell 12% in Q3" — is far more obvious here than it is buried under a chart. Drafting titles in Outline View first is the natural place to apply a BLUF (bottom-line-up-front) approach, before any visual gets attached to defend a vague one.

**3. Reorder the whole deck by dragging text, not slides.** Dragging a title line up or down in the outline moves that entire slide with it — bullets included — which is faster than juggling slide thumbnails in the normal view, especially mid-draft when you're still deciding whether the "methodology" slide belongs before or after the "key finding" slide.

**4. Demote and promote to control what becomes a bullet vs. a new slide.** `Tab` demotes a line into a bullet under the slide above it; `Shift`+`Tab` promotes a bullet back up into its own new slide with its own title. This makes it fast to test whether a point deserves its own slide or is better folded in as a supporting bullet on an existing one — a decision that's much slower to experiment with once charts and layouts are already in place.

**5. Paste a written narrative straight in.** If you've already drafted the analysis writeup in a Word doc or a Google Doc with headings, pasting it into Outline View maps headings to slide titles and body text to bullets automatically, giving you a rough first-pass deck structure in seconds instead of manually retyping section headers as slide titles one at a time.

**6. Switch back to Normal View only once the order and titles feel right.** Outline View can't show or edit charts, images, tables, or SmartArt at all — it's a planning tool, not a place to finish a deck. Once the sequence of findings reads correctly as plain text, switching to Normal View to add the actual charts and formatting means you're laying out slides you've already validated instead of discovering structural problems after the visual work is done.

The deck still needs real charts, a template, and careful chart formatting to land with an audience — but none of that is worth doing until the argument itself, in plain text, actually holds together in the right order.
