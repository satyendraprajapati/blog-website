The slowest part of a data readout is rarely the analysis — it's turning finished findings into slides. Copilot in PowerPoint can take a document or a set of notes and produce a first-pass deck, which is a genuinely useful starting point as long as you treat it as a draft, not a finished product.

**1. Start from your written analysis, not a blank deck.** Copilot's "Create presentation from file" option takes a Word document, PDF, or even a rough notes file and turns its structure into slides — section headers become slide titles, and bullet points become slide content. Writing up your findings as a short document first, even informally, gives Copilot far more to work with than typing a one-line prompt into a blank presentation.

**2. Be specific about audience and length in the prompt.** "Turn this into slides" produces something generic. "Turn this into a 6-slide deck for a leadership audience that doesn't see the raw data, ending with a recommendation slide" gives Copilot a shape to aim for, and you'll spend less time afterward deleting slides it added that nobody asked for.

**3. Use the Copilot pane to add or rework one slide at a time once the draft exists.** Rather than regenerating the whole deck for a small change, select a slide and ask Copilot to tighten it, split a dense slide into two, or rewrite a title to state the actual takeaway instead of just naming the topic. This mirrors the deck's existing structure instead of restarting from scratch.

**4. Ask it to summarize the deck before you present it.** Copilot can generate a one-paragraph summary of the whole presentation, which doubles as a sanity check — if the summary it produces doesn't match what you intended the deck to say, that's a sign the narrative isn't landing the way you think it is.

**5. Never trust the numbers it writes without checking them against your source.** Copilot works from the text and structure of your source document, and it can misread or round a figure when it rephrases a sentence. Every number that ends up on a slide — a growth rate, a dollar figure, a percentage — needs a direct check against your original analysis before the deck goes to stakeholders.

**6. Keep the chart and table data you built outside Copilot, and let it work around them.** Copilot is strongest at turning prose into slide structure, not at reproducing the exact chart you already built in Excel or Power BI. Build and link your real visuals the normal way, and use Copilot for the connective slides — framing, context, and the recommendation at the end — that tie those visuals into a story.

Copilot shortens the gap between "I've finished the analysis" and "I have a deck," but it doesn't remove the analyst's job of verifying every number and making sure the narrative actually matches the data. Treat its output as a fast first draft to edit, not a final deliverable to forward as-is.
