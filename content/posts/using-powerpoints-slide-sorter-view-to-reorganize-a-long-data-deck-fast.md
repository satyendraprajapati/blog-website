title: "Using PowerPoint's Slide Sorter View to Reorganize a Long Data Deck Fast"

excerpt: "Why Slide Sorter view is the fastest way to restructure a long data deck before a stakeholder review."
---

A 40-slide data readout rarely comes together in the order it ends up presented. Appendix slides move to the front, a chart gets cut for time, three KPI slides get grouped into one section — and doing that kind of restructuring one slide at a time in Normal view, scrolling through a long panel, is slow. Slide Sorter view is built for exactly this.

**1. Switch to it from the View tab, or just click the grid icon in the bottom-right corner of the window.** Slide Sorter replaces the single-slide editing view with a grid of every slide as a thumbnail — closer to looking at a deck of index cards laid out on a table than to editing a document. You can't edit slide content here, which is the point: it keeps you focused on structure instead of getting pulled into fixing a chart label while you're trying to reorder sections.

**2. Reordering is drag-and-drop, and multi-select works the way it does in a file browser.** Click a slide, then Ctrl+click (or Cmd+click on Mac) to add more slides to the selection, or Shift+click to grab a contiguous range. Drag the selection and every slide in it moves together, keeping its internal order — so pulling three related chart slides from the middle of a deck to right after the intro takes one drag instead of three.

**3. Sections turn a long deck into something you can actually scan.** Right-click between two slides and choose "Add Section" to break the deck into named chunks — "Context," "Key Findings," "Appendix" — each collapsible to a single header. For a deck that's grown past 30 or 40 slides over several review cycles, collapsing sections you're not currently touching cuts the visual noise down to just the slides you're working on.

**4. Deleting and duplicating are both faster here than in Normal view.** Select a slide or range and press Delete to drop it, or Ctrl+D to duplicate it in place — handy for building a "before/after" pair of the same chart with different formatting, or spinning off a backup copy of a slide before you experiment with restructuring it. Because you can see the slides on either side while you do it, it's much easier to confirm you deleted the right one than when you're navigating a long scrollable list.

**5. It's also where you set slide timings and transitions for a self-running or recorded deck.** Selecting slides in Slide Sorter view surfaces the same Transitions and Timing controls available in Normal view, but applying them to a multi-slide selection at once — useful when you want every chart slide in a section to advance after the same number of seconds for a kiosk display or a recorded walkthrough, rather than setting each one individually.

**6. Double-clicking any thumbnail drops you straight back into Normal view on that slide.** Slide Sorter isn't a separate mode you commit to — it's a quick detour for restructuring, and the moment you need to actually edit a slide's content, double-click takes you right to it without hunting through the slide panel to find where you were.

For the specific job of untangling a deck's order — moving sections around, cutting slides before a time-boxed presentation, or collapsing a sprawling appendix down to what matters — Slide Sorter does in a few drags what would otherwise be a dozen small edits in Normal view.
