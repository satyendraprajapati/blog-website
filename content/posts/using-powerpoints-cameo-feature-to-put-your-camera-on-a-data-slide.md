title: "Using PowerPoint's Cameo Feature to Put Your Camera on a Data Slide"

excerpt: "How to embed your live webcam feed directly onto a PowerPoint slide so a remote data readout feels less like a screen share."
---

When you present a data readout over a video call by sharing your screen, your camera feed usually shrinks into a tiny circle in the corner of whatever conferencing app you're using — completely disconnected from the slide it's talking about. PowerPoint's Cameo feature fixes that by putting your live webcam feed directly onto the slide itself, as an object you can resize, move, and format like any other shape.

**1. Insert it from the ribbon, not from a video call app.** On the Insert tab, choose Cameo (in the Camera group) and a placeholder for your live video feed drops onto the current slide. This is a PowerPoint feature, not a Teams or Zoom feature — the camera feed becomes part of the slide's own layout.

**2. Resize and reposition it next to the actual chart, not on top of it.** The point of Cameo is context: put your camera feed beside the KPI table or trend chart you're narrating, rather than covering the data with your own face. Snap it to a corner using PowerPoint's alignment guides so it lines up cleanly across every slide that uses it.

**3. Apply picture styles to make it match your deck.** Because Cameo treats your camera feed like an inserted picture, you can apply the same style, border, and shadow options you'd use on a photo — a rounded rectangle or subtle frame usually reads better than a plain rectangle dropped onto a data-heavy slide.

**4. Set it once on the Slide Master to keep it consistent everywhere.** Add the Cameo placeholder to a layout in Slide Master view and every slide built from that layout inherits the same camera position and size automatically, so you don't have to nudge it into place slide by slide.

**5. Turn it off for slides that need the full canvas.** Not every slide benefits from a visible presenter — a dense table or a full-bleed chart often reads better without a face competing for attention. Cameo can be toggled per slide, so use it selectively rather than leaving it on for the entire deck.

**6. It works with Slide Show and Present Live, but check your PowerPoint version first.** Cameo ships with recent Microsoft 365 versions of PowerPoint, not older perpetual-license installs, so confirm your organization's build supports it before planning a live readout around it — there's no graceful fallback if the feature simply isn't there yet.

For a remote data presentation, the format usually does more work than the content — a viewer who can see your face reacting to the chart tends to stay engaged longer than one watching a static screen share with a video thumbnail bolted on top. Cameo is a small production upgrade, but it's one of the few PowerPoint features aimed squarely at how decks get delivered rather than how they're built.
