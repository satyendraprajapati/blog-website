Formatting a chart ahead of time only gets you so far in a live readout — the moment someone asks "wait, which bar is that," you need to point at something on the screen in real time, not go back and re-edit the slide. PowerPoint's ink and laser tools do exactly that, and they only work during `Slide Show` mode, not in the editor, which is why most analysts never discover them until they need them mid-presentation.

**1. Turn the cursor into a laser pointer with `Ctrl` + click-and-hold, or `Ctrl` + `L`.** During `Slide Show` mode, holding `Ctrl` while holding the left mouse button turns the cursor into a red dot you can move across the slide — release and it reverts to a normal cursor. Pressing `Ctrl` + `L` toggles it to stay on without holding the button, useful when you need both hands on the keyboard to advance slides while still pointing.

**2. Switch pointer color under `Slide Show > Set Up Slide Show` before you present, not during.** The default red pointer is hard to see against a slide with red in the chart itself — a common problem on decline/loss charts. Pick a contrasting color (blue or green tends to hold up better against most chart palettes) from the Set Up dialog ahead of time, since there's no clean way to change it mid-presentation without breaking flow.

**3. Circle or underline a data point with the pen tool (`Ctrl` + `P`).** This switches the cursor to a freehand pen so you can circle the one bar the audience should focus on, or underline a number in a table as you say it out loud. Press `Esc` or `Ctrl` + `A` to switch back to the normal arrow cursor when you're done annotating and need to click through to the next slide.

**4. Use the highlighter (`Ctrl` + `I`) for a wider stroke over a range, not a single point.** The pen draws a thin line meant for circling one thing; the highlighter draws a broader translucent stroke better suited to sweeping across a whole row of a table or a date range on the x-axis to say "this whole quarter" rather than pointing at one value.

**5. Decide whether ink marks should persist to the next slide before you draw them.** When you advance past a slide you've annotated, PowerPoint asks whether to keep or discard the ink. Keeping it embeds those marks as objects on that slide permanently, which is useful if you're recording the session as a leave-behind but a mess if you were just circling something for the moment and don't want it baked into the deck afterward. Choosing `Discard` on that prompt is what you want most of the time in a live meeting.

**6. Erase a single stroke with `Ctrl` + `E` instead of wiping everything.** The eraser only removes the last stroke it's dragged over; `Ctrl` + `M` clears every ink mark on the current slide at once — worth knowing the difference so a mid-explanation slip of the pen doesn't force you to redo every annotation on that slide.

**7. Rehearse the ink workflow once before presenting live, not for the first time in front of stakeholders.** The keyboard shortcuts for switching between pointer, pen, and highlighter are easy to fumble under pressure the first time. Running through a practice pass — point, circle, switch back to the arrow, advance — means the mechanics are automatic by the time you're actually explaining a number that matters.

None of this replaces preparing the chart itself well; a well-highlighted bar or a clean data label still does most of the work. Ink and the laser pointer are for the moments formatting alone can't cover — an unplanned question, a comparison across two slides, a number someone in the room wants circled right now.
