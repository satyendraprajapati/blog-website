Building a data deck involves a lot of repetitive mouse work — nudging a text box a pixel at a time, duplicating a slide layout, resizing a chart to match its neighbor. None of that is analysis, and most of it is faster with a shortcut than a menu click. These are the ones that actually save time on a chart-and-table-heavy deck, not the generic "Ctrl+C" list.

**1. `Ctrl+D` to duplicate the selected object or slide.** Build one clean chart, table, or slide layout, then duplicate it instead of rebuilding formatting from scratch for every region or metric. This alone removes most of the "make it look the same as the last slide" busywork in a recurring report deck.

**2. `Ctrl+Shift+C` and `Ctrl+Shift+V` to copy and paste formatting only.** This copies font, color, size, and effects from one object to another without touching its content — useful for making a title match a template after you've pasted in new text, without redoing every formatting choice by hand.

**3. Arrow keys to nudge, `Ctrl`+arrow keys for fine pixel adjustments.** Once an object is selected, plain arrow keys move it in larger steps; holding `Ctrl` moves it in much smaller increments. That's the difference between eyeballing chart alignment and actually getting two chart edges to line up.

**4. `Shift`+drag to constrain movement or resizing to a straight line.** Holding `Shift` while dragging a chart or shape keeps it moving perfectly horizontally or vertically, and holding it while resizing keeps the aspect ratio locked — both of which matter a lot more on a chart than on decorative shapes, since a stretched chart distorts the data.

**5. `Ctrl+G` to group selected objects, `Ctrl+Shift+G` to ungroup.** A chart title, the chart itself, and a callout box are often meant to move together. Group them before repositioning, and you stop accidentally leaving one piece behind when you drag the rest.

**6. `Alt` then a letter to reach ribbon commands without leaving the keyboard.** Pressing `Alt` shows letter badges over every ribbon tab and button. It's slower to learn than a mouse click at first, but for a command you use constantly — like inserting a text box — it ends up faster once the sequence is memorized.

**7. `F5` to present from the start, `Shift+F5` to present from the current slide.** When you're checking how a specific data slide reads in presentation mode — animations, transitions, chart appearance at full size — `Shift+F5` gets you there without re-watching the intro slides every time.

**8. `Ctrl+M` to insert a new slide, `Ctrl+Enter` to move to the next placeholder.** Building a run of similar data slides — one metric per slide, for instance — goes noticeably faster when you're not reaching for the mouse between each one.

None of these shortcuts change what you're capable of building — they just remove the friction between "I know what this slide should look like" and it actually looking that way, which matters most on the decks with a dozen near-identical chart slides to produce.
