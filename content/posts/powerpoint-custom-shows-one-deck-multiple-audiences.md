title: "PowerPoint Custom Shows: One Deck, Multiple Audiences"
date: "2026-08-04"
tags: ["powerpoint", "presentation", "data-storytelling"]
excerpt: "How to maintain a single master analysis deck and present a shorter, audience-specific version from it, instead of forking a copy every time someone new needs a readout."
---

The same analysis usually needs to reach two different audiences: a 6-slide version for executives who want the headline and the recommendation, and a 30-slide version for the team that needs every chart, caveat, and methodology note behind it. Forking a separate file for each invites the two decks to quietly drift apart the next time the numbers refresh. PowerPoint's Custom Shows solve this without a second file.

**1. Build one deck with everything in it.** Keep the full analysis — summary, detail, methodology, appendix — as a single master file. Every future update to the numbers happens once, in one place, instead of needing to be copied into two or three forked versions that inevitably fall out of sync with each other.

**2. Create a Custom Show for each audience.** Go to *Slide Show → Custom Slide Show → Custom Shows → New*, give it a name like "Exec Summary," and check off only the slides that audience needs, in whatever order makes sense for them. A slide can appear in more than one Custom Show, so your headline chart can open both the exec version and the detailed version without existing twice in the file.

**3. Present a Custom Show directly, without deleting anything.** Once defined, a Custom Show runs from *Slide Show → Custom Slide Show → [name]* exactly like a normal slideshow — advancing, presenter view, and speaker notes all work the same way. Nothing gets deleted or hidden from the underlying file; you're choosing a subset to present, not editing the deck down.

**4. Use Hyperlink Custom Shows to jump between them mid-presentation.** A Custom Show can also be inserted as a clickable link on a slide (*Insert → Link → Place in This Document → Custom Shows*), which is useful for a "here's the summary — want to see the detail behind any of this?" moment live in the room. Clicking the link jumps into the full deck at the relevant slide, and a "Return to summary" link on that detail slide brings you back.

**5. Don't confuse this with Hide Slide.** *Hide Slide* removes a single slide from the *default* slideshow but still lets you jump to it manually, which is fine for occasional exceptions but doesn't scale to "maintain two distinct audience paths" — you'd need to hide and unhide slides every time you switch who you're presenting to. Custom Shows keep both paths defined permanently, so switching audiences is a menu selection, not a re-editing pass through the deck.

**6. Name Custom Shows for the audience, not the content.** "Exec Summary" and "Full Technical Review" tell you which one to pick under time pressure right before a meeting starts; "Version A" and "Version B" don't. If you present to the same few audiences repeatedly, this alone saves the minute of second-guessing that happens when you're already sharing your screen.

Once the Custom Shows are set up, updating the analysis is a single edit to the master deck, and both audiences see the update the next time you present — no re-forking, no risk of one version quietly falling a revision behind the other.
