Most analysts don't dislike PowerPoint layout work because it's hard — they dislike it because it's slow, and it eats time that should go toward the analysis instead. Designer, the pane on the *Design* tab that auto-suggests layouts, exists for exactly that gap: it takes a slide of raw content and proposes several arranged versions in seconds.

**1. Add your content first, in whatever order, then open Designer.** Drop in your chart, your key-stat text box, and a couple of bullet takeaways as plain unstyled content — Designer reads what's already on the slide and proposes layouts around it, so it needs real content to work with, not a blank slide.

**2. Open it from Design → Designer, or let it pop up automatically.** PowerPoint often opens the Design Ideas pane on its own after you paste in a chart or image; if it doesn't, Design → Designer triggers it manually. Within a few seconds, a column of thumbnail layout options appears on the right.

**3. Scan for the option that groups your chart and takeaway together, not just the prettiest one.** Designer's suggestions vary in more than just color — some center the chart, some pair it with a sidebar of stats, some pull a headline number out as a large callout. For a data slide specifically, favor the layout that visually pairs the chart with the one number or line you want the audience to walk away with, since that pairing is doing more communication work than any color choice.

**4. Click to apply, then check what happened to your chart's data labels and colors.** Applying a Designer layout can resize or recolor an inserted chart to match the slide's theme, which occasionally overrides a colorblind-friendly palette or specific data label formatting you'd already set up. Always re-check the chart after applying a suggestion rather than assuming your prior formatting survived.

**5. Regenerate suggestions after editing text, since Designer doesn't re-run automatically.** If you tighten a bullet from three lines to one after picking a layout, the spacing Designer chose was calculated for the old text length. Reopening Design → Designer refreshes the suggestions based on the current content instead of leaving a layout balanced for text that no longer exists.

**6. Skip it entirely on slides using your firm's slide master or template.** Designer works by restyling the slide's layout and placeholders, which can conflict with a locked-down corporate template or the reusable slide master you built for consistent report decks. On template-based slides, apply formatting manually so every deck stays visually consistent instead of drifting slide to slide based on whatever Designer proposed that day.

Designer won't decide what your data slide should say — that's still on you — but for the mechanical work of laying out a chart, a headline, and a couple of bullets so they don't look thrown together, it gets most slides most of the way there in under a minute.
