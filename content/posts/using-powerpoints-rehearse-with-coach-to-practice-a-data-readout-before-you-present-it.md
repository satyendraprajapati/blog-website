Most feedback on a data presentation arrives after you've already given it, when it's too late to fix the pacing or the slide you accidentally read word-for-word. PowerPoint's **Rehearse with Coach**, under Slide Show > Rehearse with Coach, runs a private practice session while you present normally, and gives you a report afterward -- no audience, no Teams call, just your microphone and camera.

**Start it like a real presentation, because it only has something to say if you talk through the whole deck.** Click Rehearse with Coach and PowerPoint drops into full-screen slide show mode with a small floating panel tracking your session. Present it exactly as you would live -- walk through each chart, say the numbers out loud, explain the takeaway on your summary slide. A 30-second skim-through doesn't give the tool enough to analyze meaningfully.

**Watch for the filler word and pacing flags, since those are the easiest wins.** The live panel counts filler words ("um," "like," "basically") and warns if you're speaking too fast or too slowly for the moment -- both common when a data presenter defaults to narrating every axis label instead of stating the one number that matters. For a dense KPI slide, slowing down on the single headline figure and speeding past the methodology footnote usually reads better than an even pace across both.

**Pay closer attention to the "reading from slide" warning than you might expect to.** Coach flags when your spoken words closely match the text on screen, which is a specific risk on data slides: it's tempting to just read out the bullet under a chart instead of walking the audience through what the chart is actually showing. If you get this warning repeatedly, it's usually a sign the slide has too much text on it rather than that you need to memorize a script -- cut the bullet down to a label and say the insight instead.

**Use the originality and inclusiveness checks if your deck goes to a broad audience.** Coach also flags potentially non-inclusive phrasing and, in some versions, offers a check for monotone delivery over a long stretch -- both relevant for a quarterly results deck going to a mixed internal audience rather than a technical readout among analysts who already know the shorthand.

**Read the summary report as a punch list, not a grade.** After you finish, Coach produces a report with your pace (words per minute), filler word count, and a timeline of where each flag occurred, tied to the specific slide. Rerun the rehearsal focusing only on the two or three slides with the most flags rather than redoing the whole deck -- for a 20-slide data readout, the problems are usually concentrated on the slide with the most complex chart, not spread evenly throughout.

It won't catch whether your DAX measure is actually correct or whether the chart you picked is the right one for the data, but it catches the delivery problems that undermine good analysis in a live readout -- and it does it before a stakeholder is the one giving you that feedback.
