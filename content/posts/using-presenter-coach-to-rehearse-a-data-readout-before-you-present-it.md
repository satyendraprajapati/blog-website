---

Speaker Notes and Presenter View help you *deliver* a data readout without reading the slides — but they don't tell you beforehand whether your pacing is off or you're leaning on "um" every third sentence. That's what Presenter Coach does: a private rehearsal tool that scores a practice run before an actual audience ever sees the deck.

**1. Launch it from *Slide Show → Rehearse with Coach*.** It runs like a normal slide show, except a sidebar tracks your speech in real time and a summary report generates the moment you end the show. Run through the full deck once, at the pace you'd actually use live — rehearsing at double speed to save time just means the feedback doesn't reflect how you'll actually sound.

**2. Watch for the pacing flag on data-heavy slides specifically.** Coach tracks words-per-minute and flags when you're speaking too fast or too slow for the moment. On a slide with a dense chart, a fast pace usually means you're rushing past the number the audience needs a second to actually process — slow down deliberately on the slide with the finding, even if you speed back up on context slides.

**3. Treat the filler-word count as a signal, not a scorecard to zero out.** Coach counts "um," "like," and other verbal fillers per run. A few are normal and don't read as unprepared in the room — what's worth fixing is a spike concentrated on one specific slide, which usually means you don't actually have a clean explanation for that number yet and are stalling while you think of one live.

**4. Use the "reading from slide" flag to catch slides that are still doing your talking for you.** Coach detects when your spoken words closely match the on-screen text, which is the concrete, measurable version of "don't read your slides." If it flags a slide repeatedly across rehearsals, the fix usually isn't better delivery — it's cutting text off the slide until there's nothing left to read verbatim, which pushes you back toward the Speaker Notes approach of talking through context that isn't already visible.

**5. Check the sensitive-phrases warning if the deck is going to a broad audience.** Coach flags language that can land badly with a general audience — informal phrasing, culturally specific references, or language assuming context a mixed audience won't have. Worth a look before a readout going to stakeholders outside your immediate team, less relevant for a rehearsal in front of people who already share your context.

**6. Re-run it after fixing what it flagged, not just once.** The first pass usually surfaces two or three things — a slide you're reading verbatim, a pacing dip after the agenda slide, a filler-word spike on the one number you're least confident in. Fix those specific spots and rehearse again rather than treating one run as a pass/fail gate; the score matters less than whether the same issue shows up a second time.

**7. Don't rehearse the whole deck every time once you're close.** Once Coach stops flagging anything new across a full run, isolate just the slides that still feel shaky — jump to them directly in *Rehearse with Coach* rather than running the entire deck end to end each time, which wastes rehearsal time re-practicing slides that were already fine.

Coach won't tell you whether the analysis itself is right, and it won't replace practicing in front of an actual person before something high-stakes. What it catches is the mechanical stuff — pace, filler words, reading off the slide — that's otherwise invisible to you until someone in the room notices it first.
