---

Most monthly or quarterly analysis decks reuse a handful of slides from the last cycle — the methodology slide, a KPI summary layout, a chart that just needs updated numbers. The obvious move is to copy the slide from the old file and paste it into the new one, but that carries the old deck's theme, fonts, and colors along with it, so the pasted slide looks visibly different from everything around it until you manually reformat it. Reuse Slides is built specifically to avoid that.

**1. Open it from Home → New Slide → Reuse Slides.** This opens a pane on the right where you browse to another `.pptx` file. PowerPoint shows thumbnails of every slide in that file without inserting anything yet, so you can see what's available before committing to any of it.

**2. Check "Keep source formatting" — or don't — before you click a single slide.** This checkbox at the bottom of the pane is the entire point of the feature. Leave it unchecked and every slide you import gets restyled to match your *current* presentation's theme (fonts, colors, layout placeholders) automatically. Check it, and the slide keeps its exact original look, colors and all — the right choice only when you're deliberately bringing in a client's branded template slide rather than your own report content.

**3. Click each thumbnail to insert it at the current position.** Slides insert one at a time where your cursor was in the slide panel, in the order you click them — there's no multi-select, so for six or seven slides you're clicking through each one, but each import is instant and it beats manually rebuilding a KPI table from scratch.

**4. Re-check chart data links after reusing a slide with a chart.** A reused slide with an embedded or linked chart brings its original data source along with it, not a live connection to your current workbook. Right-click the chart → Edit Data to confirm it's pointing at this month's numbers rather than quietly still displaying last month's, since the slide will look correct at a glance either way.

**5. Reuse from a whole deck, not just a section, when you're building a template library.** If you maintain a "greatest hits" file of reusable report slides — a title slide, a methodology page, a standard KPI layout — Reuse Slides works just as well pointed at that reference deck as it does pointed at last month's actual report, which is a cleaner source than hunting through old project files for the slide you remember building once.

**6. Expect placeholder text and alt text to carry over.** Speaker notes, alt text on images and charts, and any placeholder labels come in with the slide. That's usually what you want for methodology or template slides, but worth a quick scan on data slides — an old alt text description or a speaker note referencing last quarter's numbers is easy to miss since it isn't visible on the slide itself.

Once you're in the habit of building from a small set of reusable source slides instead of a copy-paste chain of old files, a new week's report starts from something close to finished instead of a blank deck with a chart pasted into the wrong theme.
