Sharing your screen to present a data deck in a Teams call works, but every viewer is stuck at your zoom level, your window size, and whatever's cluttering your desktop if you switch apps mid-call. PowerPoint Live is Teams' built-in alternative: it hands each attendee their own rendered copy of the deck instead of a video feed of yours.

**1. Start it from Teams' Share menu, not by opening PowerPoint first.** In a Teams meeting, click *Share*, and under "PowerPoint Live" pick the file — either from recent files, OneDrive, or by browsing to it. This uploads the deck into the meeting itself rather than sharing your local screen, so viewers get real slide content instead of a compressed video stream of it.

**2. Let attendees navigate independently without derailing the group view.** By default everyone follows your slide changes, but each viewer can click ahead or back to review a slide you've already passed — useful when someone wants to re-read a chart's footnote instead of interrupting to ask you to go back. Presenter Mode lets you bring the group back in sync at any point without losing your place.

**3. Point out the High Contrast and screen-reader-friendly rendering for accessibility.** Because Teams renders the deck rather than streaming your screen, each viewer can toggle a high-contrast display mode and use a screen reader against the actual slide content — neither of which works against a shared-screen video feed. If a data deck needs to reach a genuinely mixed-accessibility audience, this alone is a reason to prefer Live over screen share.

**4. Use the built-in live captions per-viewer language setting for a mixed-language audience.** Captions in PowerPoint Live can be translated per attendee — someone can read live captions in a different language than the one you're presenting in, without you doing anything differently on your end. For a data readout going to a multi-region team, this removes a real barrier without extra prep work.

**5. Keep Speaker Notes visible to you but hidden from attendees, same as a normal slide show.** PowerPoint Live respects Presenter View — your notes stay on your screen only, so you can still talk through the "why" behind a number without attendees seeing the notes pane. Combine this with a deck built around BLUF slide titles so the takeaway is visible even to someone skimming ahead on their own.

**6. Fall back to a plain screen share for anything Live doesn't render well.** Embedded video, complex custom animations, and some third-party add-in content don't always translate cleanly through PowerPoint Live's rendering. Do a quick run-through of an unfamiliar deck in a test meeting before presenting it live to a real audience, rather than discovering a rendering gap mid-readout.

For any recurring remote data presentation — a weekly regional readout, a monthly stakeholder review — PowerPoint Live turns what used to be a shared video feed into something every attendee can actually navigate, read, and follow on their own terms.
